1The Question

For each of the 514 kab/kota in Indonesia, the app gives the chance of rain in the next 1, 2 and 3 hours. A new forecast comes every 30 minutes.

"Rain" has one exact meaning here. The rain rate must reach 0.5 mm/h in at least one half-hour of the window. A light shower over one corner of a large kabupaten can stay below that value.

A short-range forecast like this is a . It is hard for two reasons. Many rain clouds form in place and grow within an hour or two, so a forecast cannot follow a cloud that does not exist yet. Indonesia also has no open weather radar archive, so the system uses satellites only.

Over 10 years, a 1 hour window had rain in 20.2% of cases, a 2 hour window in 25.6% and a 3 hour window in 30.0%.

More detail
  • The stored rain value is rounded to 0.1 mm/h, so in practice an area mean just above 0.45 mm/h counts as rain.
  • In the 2-day smoke test, the scores fell at about 08:00 WIB, when new storms formed instead of moving in.

2The Answer in One Picture

The system turns satellite images into a few numbers per area, gives these numbers to a model, and colours the map with the model's probability. About 5 hours later the satellite rain estimate arrives, and each forecast gets a score.

  1. Satellites and BMKGHimawari-9 sees the cloud tops every 10 minutes, NASA IMERG estimates the rain every 30 minutes, and BMKG publishes its own forecast twice a day.
  2. Numbers per areaEach grid becomes a few numbers per kab/kota, such as the share of very cold cloud.
  3. ModelThree models, one for each , read 25 numbers per area.
  4. ProbabilityEach area gets one chance of rain for the next 1 hour, 2 hours and 3 hours.
  5. Map and scoreThe app colours each area, and later compares each forecast with the rain that IMERG observed.

4From Grids to Kab/Kota

The system turns each satellite grid into one row per kab/kota. A grid cell counts in proportion to the share of the cell that lies inside the area.

The areas are 519 polygons from geoBoundaries. 514 are admin kab/kota. Five are lakes, a reservoir and a forest (Danau, Danau Toba, Hutan, Waduk Cirata, Wadung Kedungombo). The map draws them, but the model does not forecast them, because they have no Kemendagri code and no BMKG point.

The lists, for each area, the cells it touches and a weight from 0 to 1: the share of that cell's area inside the polygon. The area mean is the sum of weight times value, divided by the sum of the weights.

A simpler rule, "use the cells whose centre is inside", leaves 19 areas with no cell at all, among them 9 of the 10 smallest. The weighted rule gives every one of the 514 areas a value. Areas touch 1 to 441 IMERG cells (median 32.5) and 5 to 2,961 MERGIR cells (median 169).

From the rain grid, each area gets the mean rate, the highest cell and the . From the cloud grid, each area gets the coldest cell, the mean brightness temperature, the and three motion values (section 7).

Cells as a table
More detail
  • The weights use the full-resolution polygons. The drawing uses the simplified map copy, so a drawn edge can differ a little from the edge in the calculation.
  • The weighted cell areas differ from the polygon areas by 0.44% to 0.45% for every area. The cell areas use a sphere and the polygon areas use the WGS84 ellipsoid. A mean does not change, because the same factor is in the top and the bottom.
  • The rain image stores each cell as a whole number of 0.1 mm/h up to 25.5 mm/h, and a missing cell as 0. So the value computed here can differ a little from the stored one.

5The Pipeline

Data moves through three storage zones. keeps raw files for hours to days, keeps small per-area tables for 10 years, and keeps the forecasts and scores that the app reads. A built 10 years of history once, and a adds new data as it lands.

Highlight

    The Big Data View

    The course describes big data with five Vs (Pertemuan 1). This system meets each one:

    Volume
    The system has processed … IMERG values and … MERGIR values. The backfills downloaded 88.5 GB and deleted each file after use. Silver keeps … of per-area tables.
    Velocity
    A new Himawari scan comes every 10 minutes (about 9 MB for the three segments), a new IMERG slot every 30 minutes, and a new BMKG run twice a day.
    Variety
    HDF5 and NetCDF4 grids, Himawari's binary format, JSON, pipe-separated text, METAR report text and GeoJSON.
    Veracity
    IMERG is checked against airport reports. Each backfill month gets a gap report: 0 missing slots in 130 months. BMKG records that fail a check go to a .
    Value
    A rain chance and an alert level per kab/kota, compared with BMKG's own forecast.

    The design follows the (Pertemuan 2). The batch layer is the 10-year backfill, the feature build and the training. The speed layer is the ticks that react to each new IMERG slot, Himawari scan and BMKG run. The serving layer is gold Parquet, read by DuckDB behind FastAPI. A Kappa design (one stream path) does not fit, because the 10 years of history come from MERGIR, a different product from the live Himawari stream.

    Storage follows the zones of Pertemuan 4. Tables are files with compression. Small integer codes keep them small: *_x10 stores a value times 10 as int16, and *_pct stores a share from 0 to 100 as uint8. Time tables are split into monthly (year=YYYY/month=MM). The job deletes whole partitions older than their window, and a disk check fails above ….

    More detail
    • Batch run times: IMERG backfill 3 h 50 min (189,936 slots), MERGIR backfill 6 h 22 min (189,936 frames), feature build 539 s (22,143,120 rows), training 1,910 s, peak memory 4.6 GB.
    • Spark runs in local mode with 3 worker threads on one machine (8 cores, 7.7 GB RAM). The same code can run on a cluster.
    • holds the job definitions: for BMKG (every 15 min), IMERG (10 min) and Himawari (5 min), and schedules for METAR (hourly), retention (daily) and clean-up (weekly). On the dev machine the live jobs run as shell loops for now: installing the timers or a Dagster daemon waits for a decision.
    • Replication: a nightly copy of the raw BMKG files, silver and the models to a second disk is planned for the server move. It is not built yet.

      7The Prediction

      Three LightGBM models, one per lead, read 25 numbers about an area at the and give a probability. They learned from 8 years of history and were tested on later years they never saw.

      What the Model Knows at Issue Time

      The model must see exactly what the live system sees. This is the main rule of the design.

      InputNewest data at issue time tWhy
      CloudsThe scan at tHimawari lands about 12 to 15 min after the scan starts
      IMERG rainThe slot that starts 360 min before tIMERG lands about 5 h late. 360 min covers the 30 min slot plus the delay
      BMKGNot an inputNo past runs exist, so 10-year training cannot include it. It is a baseline
      ClimatologyTraining years only (2016 to 2023)Later years must stay unseen

      Features in Groups

      • Cloud now: cold_pct (share colder than 233 K), tb_min (coldest cell), tb_mean, cold_trend_1h (change of cold_pct in the last hour), nb_cold_pct (mean of the neighbours).
      • Cloud motion: finds how the cold cloud moved in the last half-hour. The system moves the cold cloud forward by 1, 2 and 3 hours (): adv1_pct, adv2_pct, adv3_pct are the shares of the area that the moved cloud covers. Also adv_max_pct and flow_kmh (mean speed of the cold cloud).
      • Recent rain (6 h old): rain_lag, wet_lag, rainmax_lag at the newest usable slot, rain totals in mm over 3, 6 and 24 h up to that slot, and nb_rain_lag (neighbours).
      • Time: the local hour and the day of the year, each as a sine and a cosine, so 23:30 sits next to 00:00.
      • Place: area_km2 and island.
      • Climatology: clim_wet_pct and clim_p, the share of past windows with rain for this area, month and local hour ().

      Neighbours are areas with a shared border, plus the 6 nearest areas for the 30 areas with no land border.

      How the Model Learns

      LightGBM builds many small decision trees, one after another. Each new tree corrects part of the error that the trees before it left. Each model here has 2,000 trees of at most 15 leaves, with learning rate 0.03.

      The data is split by time, never at random, because two half-hours next to each other are almost the same row.

      More detail
      • Most of the skill comes from the clouds now. Share of the total gain at +1 h: cold_pct 46%, tb_mean 26%, tb_min 14%. At +3 h the motion values carry 17%. The chart in section 8 shows all leads.
      • In a one-month test, removing the cloud-now group cut the Brier skill score by 0.066, 0.058 and 0.053 at +1, +2 and +3 h. Removing the recent-rain group cut it by 0.003 or less.
      • The motion settings (OpenCV DIS "fast", 33 km smoothing) won a test of 8 settings over one week. They cut the error of the moved cold cloud share by 14.4%, 8.7% and 5.1% against "no motion" at +1, +2 and +3 h.
      • All three models reached the 2,000-tree cap. A test with 8,000 trees at +1 h added only 0.004 to the validation Brier skill score, so the cap stays.
      • The 360 min IMERG lag cost 0.003 to 0.005 Brier skill score against a 300 min lag in the one-month test. 300 min would use a slot that is not yet available live.

      8How Good Is It

      On 2025, a year the model never saw, its is … better than climatology for +1, +2 and +3 h. The two simple rules, and motion only, score worse than climatology.

      A probability forecast needs probability scores. Accuracy alone does not work: most windows are dry, so "always dry" gets a high accuracy.

      • The Brier score is the mean squared error of the probability (0 is perfect).
      • The (BSS) compares it with climatology: 0 means as good as the long-term rate, 1 means perfect, and below 0 means worse than climatology.
      • measures how well the probability ranks wet windows above dry ones (0.5 is chance, 1 is perfect).
      • counts hits divided by hits, misses and false alarms, at a yes or no cut of 50%. is the share of wet windows that the forecast caught. is the share of rain forecasts that stayed dry.

      Lead

      After the IMERG calibration change (2026-03 to 2026-10), the BSS is lower. These months are drier, and the 2016 to 2023 climatology does not know the drier V07C estimates.

      BMKG cannot be scored on history, because BMKG publishes no past runs. The live comparison started with the archive on 2026-11-20. On a 48 h replay (2026-11-18 02:00 to 11-19 23:00 UTC) with Himawari input, the live model scored BSS 0.388, 0.340 and 0.284.       9The Live Loop

      Every 30 minutes a Himawari scan starts a forecast cycle. The forecast is ready about 15 minutes after the scan time, and its check comes about 5 hours after its window ends.

      1. tHimawari-9 starts a full-disk scan.
      2. About t + 13 minThe three Band 13 segments over Indonesia land (live median …).
      3. Within a minuteThe scorer builds 514 feature rows and writes 1,542 probabilities, 514 areas times 3 leads (last delay after t: …).
      4. Within about 2 minThe map frames are built again, and the web app sees them within 60 s.
      5. About 5 h after the windowIMERG covers the window. marks each forecast as wet or dry, and the daily scores update.

      At issue time t, the newest rain that the model knows is 6 hours old. The clouds are fresh. So the clouds carry the forecast, and IMERG rain is mostly the check afterwards.

      Hatched: half-hours that the model could not know at t. The yellow line is t. A window outline is solid when it rained and dashed when it stayed dry.

      More detail
      • An issue happens only for scans at :00 and :30 that also have a scan 1 hour earlier (for cold_trend_1h).
      • The BMKG run in force is the newest run first seen at or before t. Its first-seen time is accurate to about 1 hour, because of BMKG's cache.
      • Training used one issue per hour to keep the table small. Live, every half-hour is an issue.

      10Limits and Honest Caveats

      The truth itself is a satellite estimate, the newest rain is 6 hours old, and an area mean can hide a local storm. The live record is still short.

      • IMERG is an estimate, not a gauge. It misses some heavy rain over mountains. The same estimate is the training truth and the score truth, so the scores measure agreement with IMERG. Against airport reports (January 2025, 53 stations), an hour counts as wet for IMERG when the airport's area mean reaches 0.5 mm/h in either half-hour. IMERG caught 71% of the reported rain hours, and 65% of its wet hours had no rain in the report (CSI 0.31). A point report and an area mean measure different things, so part of this gap is expected.
      • Latency. IMERG lands about 5 hours late, so the model never sees the last 6 hours of rain.
      • New storms. A satellite sees a cloud only after it forms. The smoke test lost skill at about 08:00 WIB, when new storms formed in place.
      • The area mean hides local storms. A storm over one cell of a large kabupaten (up to 441 cells) barely moves the mean. A small kota such as Kota Malang has only 4 cells.
      • Two cloud sources. Training used MERGIR and live uses Himawari-9. The check of their difference waits for MERGIR to cover the live days (about 47 h late).
      • BMKG comparison. BMKG is read at one village per kab/kota. The time that a 3 h BMKG step covers is assumed centred on its time stamp, and is not yet tested. Three areas in Papua Barat Daya (Raja Ampat, Sorong Selatan, Maybrat) use a point from another area inside their polygon, because BMKG rejects their own codes.
      • Separate models per lead. The +2 h window contains the +1 h window, so p1 must not be above p2. In 1.4% of live cases it is (by at most 6 points).
      • One machine. The live jobs run as shell loops on the dev machine, so a restart stops them until someone starts them again.
      • A future product change. IMERG Early will move to version 08, and the model must be trained again then.

      11Glossary

      Nowcast
      A short-range forecast made mostly from what is visible now. Here: the next 1, 2 and 3 hours.
      Lead time
      The length of the forecast window from the issue time: 1, 2 or 3 hours here. Each lead has its own model.
      Issue time
      The half-hour slot t at which a forecast starts. All windows begin at t. The forecast is written about 15 minutes later.
      Slot
      A half-hour period named by its start time in UTC, for example 03:30 for 03:30 to 04:00.
      Kab/kota
      Kabupaten (regency) or kota (city), the second level of Indonesian government. Indonesia has 514.
      Area mean
      The weighted mean of all grid cells that touch the area, weighted by the share of each cell inside it.
      Membership table
      A table of area, cell and weight. The weight is the share of the cell's area inside the area's polygon.
      Wet share
      The weighted share of cells with at least 0.1 mm/h.
      IMERG Early
      The "Early" run of NASA's IMERG product (GPM_3IMERGHHE): rain every 30 minutes on a 0.1° grid, about 5 hours after the fact.
      MERGIR
      GPM_MERGIR: merged geostationary infrared at about 4 km every 30 minutes, back to 1998. About 47 hours late.
      Himawari Band 13
      The infrared channel of the Advanced Himawari Imager on Himawari-9. It gives the brightness temperature of the cloud tops every 10 minutes.
      Brightness temperature (Tb)
      The temperature that matches the infrared energy the satellite receives. Cold tops (high clouds) give low values.
      Cold cloud share
      The weighted share of cells colder than 233 K (about -40 °C). It is the model's strongest input.
      Advection
      Here: moving the cold cloud of now along its measured motion, to guess where it will be 1, 2 and 3 hours later.
      Optical flow
      A method that finds how each part of an image moved between two frames. The system uses OpenCV DIS.
      Climatology
      The share of past windows with rain for this area, month and local hour, from 2016 to 2023. It is the baseline for the Brier skill score.
      Persistence
      A baseline that forecasts rain if the newest usable IMERG slot (6 hours old) had rain.
      Bronze
      The storage zone for files as they were downloaded. Windows from 6 hours to 12 months.
      Silver
      The storage zone for checked tables with one row per area and half-hour. Kept for 10 years.
      Gold
      The storage zone for forecasts, verification, scores and map frames.
      Batch layer
      The part of a Lambda design that processes all history at once: here the 10-year backfills, features and training.
      Speed layer
      The part of a Lambda design that handles each new file in minutes: here the IMERG, Himawari and BMKG ticks and the live scorer.
      Lambda architecture
      A design with one batch path for history and one stream path for new data, joined in a serving layer.
      Sensor
      In Dagster, a check that starts a job when new data appears, for example a new IMERG granule.
      Dagster
      A tool that defines the jobs, their inputs and outputs, and when they run.
      Spark
      Apache Spark. It builds the feature table with window functions. Here it runs in local mode on one machine.
      Partition
      A part of a table stored in its own folder, for example year=2026/month=11. Old data is deleted folder by folder.
      Retention
      The window after which the retention job deletes a partition. Each dataset has one.
      Dead-letter folder
      A folder for records that fail validation, kept for 3 months so they can be checked and processed again.
      Parquet
      A compressed file format that stores each column together, so a query reads only the columns it needs.
      ZSTD
      Zstandard, the compression in every Parquet file here.
      Feature
      One column the model reads, for example cold_pct. Each area and issue time has 25.
      LightGBM
      A library that builds gradient-boosted decision trees. Each new tree corrects part of the error of the trees before it.
      Probability of rain
      The model's chance, from 0 to 100%, that the area mean reaches 0.5 mm/h in at least one half-hour of the window.
      Base rate
      The share of windows in a data set that had rain.
      Brier score
      The mean of (probability minus outcome) squared, with outcome 1 for rain and 0 for dry. 0 is perfect.
      Brier skill score (BSS)
      1 minus the model's Brier score divided by the Brier score of climatology. 0 is as good as climatology, 1 is perfect.
      AUC
      Area under the ROC curve: the chance that a random wet window gets a higher probability than a random dry one.
      CSI
      Critical success index: hits / (hits + misses + false alarms), for a yes or no cut.
      POD
      Probability of detection: hits / (hits + misses).
      FAR
      False alarm ratio: false alarms / (hits + false alarms).
      Reliability
      A forecast is reliable when, of all windows forecast at 30%, about 30% rain. The reliability chart shows this in 10 bins.
      Hindcast
      A forecast run for a past issue time with only the data that was available then. It filled the map before live scoring started.
      Verification
      Comparing each forecast with IMERG once IMERG covers its whole window.
      BMKG
      Badan Meteorologi, Klimatologi, dan Geofisika. Its public API gives forecasts in 3 hour steps.
      ENSO
      El Niño Southern Oscillation, a cycle of the tropical Pacific Ocean. The History page tags El Niño and La Niña years from NOAA's Relative Oceanic Niño Index.
      Dekad
      A period of 10 days, used for wet-season timing on the History page.
      WIB, WITA, WIT
      Western (UTC+7), Central (UTC+8) and Eastern (UTC+9) Indonesia Time. The app shows times in WIB.
      Underlined term
      Each underlined term opens a short definition. Its full entry is in this list.
